The Early Years

As a child I was always making something. Wether taking familiar shaped stick and rubber banding them to my wrists to pretend they were spiderman webshooters, to recreateing my favorite show characters out of construction paper. I wanted to be an artist through most of my elementary years, then at some point I think in 3rd grade, by Dad showed me how to build an electro magnet out of some copper wire, and an old peice of iron coupled with an old morse code key as the switch and a large 9v battery to power it. All the sudden I wanted to be an electrical engineer, and with my love of sci-fi, particularly Star Wars at the time, I was convinced when I was in my 20's I would be smare enough to create a light saber. The electiral engineer dream lasted through highschool electronics class where I built a radar gun out of some old coffee cans and spare parts that had accumulated over the years. This changed a bit after the teacher started teachin comptia A+ and I realized I knew a lot more about computers and operating systems than most students. I realized I could make a lot of money by creating websites as web 2.0 was rising quickly, however I never was in love with the idea, just that as I was getting older I realized that money was going to be the major controlling factor in life so I should try something im already good at that was currently in high demand. Through the years I kept at it, developing a backend system to manage finances and supply of a small shaved ice business that I owned through my college years, with PhP and MySql. I turned this in as a final for a CS101 class I was enrolled in. While most of the student struggled and where expecting an HTML class, the professor was very current with the days tech, and wanted to teach what was in the real world. At this point I realized I had a knack for systems implementation and design, with the shaved ice being a seaonsal buisness, I started looking for IT student jobs within the campus. I was offered a job as a student worker for the infrastructure team, and after several years I was able to satisfy my aptitude for both creativity and technology by desining and implmenting proof of concepts for systems like Virtual Desktop Infrastructure. That is until I took a trip to India in 2006.

Photographic Awakening

During the trip to India, there was much to photograph. At the time I had a 2.1 megapixel Canon Elph point and shoot. I thought I was pretty good as I had dabbled in photography, I was my middle school newspaper's photographer, and in my early teens I had a job photogrphay cars for a large used car dealership in the area to assist in online sales. However, after taking a few snaps of the Taj Mahal from a view within the Red Fort, another tourist walked up and pointed out that there was an excellend star pattern in the brick, and that I should line the Taj through it and take a creative shot. At this point I realized that I wasnt opening my eyes to what was right there and how many beautiful options for such things where present in the world that I was clearly ignoring. I was only 20 and I had a lot of other things on my mind so you can really blame me. While this was a turning point for me, it didnt sligshot my photography skills, but rather my attention to the world around me. As the yeas passed I got more advance camera gear, and did a lot of automotive photography as I was super in to cars & performance modifications. The Fast and the Furious was a big influence in the area I was living and there was a large car scene in the city. However I like to think I was more balance, spending some weekends a the local road course volunteering as a grid marshall to get some free track instruction. I spent a lot of time photographing the racers, the cars, and the action, but nothing too creative. I would sometimes learn different photoshop skills and create blends of my friends cars with transparencies to the hoods so you could see the turbochargers underneath, for the online car forums. This was the mainstay of my photographic journey until the car scene started to both die, and become too expensive to maintain with adult life and things like mortgages. While I continued to take photos, they were mostly limited to my phone, capturing moments that had some sort of significance to me, the way afternoon light was bouncing off my living room floor and landing right on the face of a buddha bust that I had next to my front door to remember the way I felt in the first house that I had owned etc. Looking back on them now, I can remember how I felt at the time, but the phone cameras just didnt capture what I really wanted, but the memories are there, and they carried forward into my photographic philiosophies of today.

Astrophotography

Coming home from work day in and day out, trying to escape the cold winter in 2016, I decided to re-watch Carl Sagans Cosmos, followed by the reboot with Neil Degrasse Tyson. This forced me to purchase my first electronically controlled telescope, that had a dedicated port for a DSLR (Celestrono Nexstar 4SE). At first I didnt really want to take any photographs, the DSLR part was just a nice to have. After the first few nights of obsercation however, everything changed. I got so into photographing what I was seeing that I decided to get creative with technology again and started using a Raspberry Pi with a small camera attache to both control the movements of my telescope while live streaming the video feed of Jupiter or Mars to YouTube. I was quickly hooked, and ended up selling much of the equipment after researching and discovering better solutions to photograph galaxies and deep space objects. Still using the Raspberry Pi to help align my mount and track the objects as the earth moved, I was able to really grasp observing the deep universe. Luckily I lived in an area that was fairly dark on the Bortle scale, I was able to do this from the back yard night in and night out even in the winter as total remote control over the equpiment made it so I didnt need to freeze for the photos. Alas due to some other contstraints that were out of my control, I ended up moving to Indianapois, where I didnt have dark sky advantage. While I would still take my equipment out to rural areas in the warmer seasons, it became more of a chore to safely haul everything out in to the country, and make sure I always had enough battery power to sustain being out for several hours at a time. At this point Astophotography took a back seat for the time being. I still participate occasionally, and have some goals of a cabin or tiny home out in the dark regions of the country, but for now deepspace and milky way astropphotography is reseverved for long weekend road trips outside of LA county. If you would like to see some of my astro work check out my Astrobin gallery. They are very generous about hosting exteremely large files that have hours worth of long expsure data.
